# Planning and page review

## Six review perspectives

Use the perspectives needed for the business. These are questions for the current author, not independent reviewers or automatic agents.

| Perspective | Questions that change the proposal | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Planning / municipal buyer | What existing problem, why this intervention, what decision now? | Problem→scope→buyer value→next decision |
| Locality / urban context | Where is demand plausible, what local evidence supports it, what remains to survey? | Real map, candidate basis, hold conditions |
| Field safety | Who sees/hears/acts; what happens if detection fails? | Field response, responsible actor, fallback and recovery evidence |
| Product / systems / smart city | What does each product do itself; what crosses interfaces? | Product functions, data/control paths, states and ownership boundaries |
| Visual / editorial design | What must be seen rather than read? Can it be read at presentation scale? | Semantic visual, layout zones, hierarchy, aligned labels and connectors |
| Commercial delivery | Why buy this integrated offer; who operates it; what remains after the pilot? | Fair comparison, scope/quote, lifecycle costs, acceptance and reuse |

Record an actionable finding, supporting evidence/unknown, affected pages and resulting edit. If evidence is absent, author a research/field task or conditional proposal. Do not fabricate a consensus, local installation, specification or score.

## AI solution explanation

For AI proposals, each application page identifies the observed input, learned inference task, resulting estimate, deterministic event/control conditions, recipient/action and validation limits. Distinguish AI inference from LED playback, thresholds, transmission and human judgment. Technical pages explain target-device compatibility, model/version deployment and rollback, data separation, condition-specific error evaluation and physical-output verification. Put these in structured authoring inputs and actual editable page marks; notes alone do not repair a missing explanation. Do not imply a model, supplier integration, accuracy or runtime specification has been delivered when it is only proposed.

## Visual selection by reader task

| Reader task | Main encoding | Insufficient substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Locate / prioritize places | Georeferenced map with bounds, scale, attribution and coordinate provenance | Relationship diagram or locality names in cards |
| Compare quantities | Native chart with unit, period, geography, baseline and assumptions | Decorative percentages or unsupported gauges |
| Understand a field intervention | Scene with equipment, target audience, sightline/direction and editable annotations | Generic gate/camera image |
| Understand behavior / failure | Actor lanes or state paths: trigger→response→owner→outcome | Arrows between vague nouns |
| Understand system scope | Product and actor boundaries with explicit data/action paths | Undifferentiated component boxes |
| Choose an alternative | Same criteria and supported evidence across columns | Separate vendor brochures or fabricated ranking |
| Check schedule / reuse | Canonical duration ranges or asset lifecycle with acceptance | Equally sized text cards pretending to encode duration |
| Evaluate expected value | Cause→measure→field response→hypothesis→measurement | Outcome slogan, guessed savings, photos without mechanism |
| Identify proposer / contact | Real supplied logo, legal name and source-linked contacts | Invented logo or a forced process diagram |

A well-structured comparison table can be the right visual. Repeated explanatory text tables need a closer check: can the viewer see a relationship, state, boundary or sequence without reading every cell? If not, revise the composition. Avoid forcing pictures onto already clear charts, maps or tables.

## Page brief

Author `question`, `answer`, `title`, proof claims and limitation. Then set `visualPlan`:

- `intent`, `kind`, `encoding`, nodes/edges and the actual map/chart/scene/schedule contract.
- `explanation.readerTask`: the decision or understanding this page supports.
- `howToRead`: what visible relation the viewer should follow.
- `notProven`: the material boundary between concept, evidence, expectation and achievement.
- `requiredMarks`: minimum real marks the renderer must produce, not a visual quality score.
- `readingGuide`: one short primary message, exact key phrase and a few focus phrases. Do not emphasize everything.

Write body copy after the visual brief. Keep essential qualifications visible; put detailed sources, formulas and internal procedure in the dossier. Fix the story if it depends on reading several paragraphs before understanding the picture.

## Evidence and decision gates

Regional observations do not establish site demand. Public plans do not establish live construction, control rights, purchase intent or revenue. Locality reference coordinates are not surveyed parcels or gates. Generated scenes must not impersonate a real surveyed site.

Charts must preserve missingness, population and period; a total and its subset must not be stacked. Targets and hypotheses are not measured achievements. A zero eligible denominator means not calculated. Gross time saved must subtract verification, false alarms and added operation work before claiming net benefit; unknown work is not zero.

The executive brief connects scope, schedule and today's decision. Include canonical cost/status when it belongs to the requested audience and scope; an explicit request to omit costs removes cost pages, amounts and fee/ROI discussion from visible copy and notes, while preserving original estimates in private source history. Candidate gates require observations and hold conditions. Schedule stages need deliverables, acceptance evidence and proposed reviewers. Failure paths need detection, action, owner and resumption evidence. Keep field safety responsibilities explicit and distinguish proposed assignments from agreed contracts.

For reusable equipment, include installation, operation, recovery, transport, repair, custody/storage, idle time, configuration/data cleanup, next-site installation and reacceptance in equal-scope comparisons. An expanded scope needs a new quote. Do not divide purchase price by assumed reuse count to promise savings.

## Review record and completion

Keep one row per actual page: number/id, buyer question, main visual, retained/changed/deferred decision, reason and inspected output path. Record observed defects, not a generic checklist marked pass. A contact sheet is navigation only; read actual pages at usable scale. Inspect all changed pages and every page after shared style changes. Verify crops, image orientation, actor labels, routes, source conditions, text contrast and product visibility.

Run type checks, targeted tests, PPTX/PDF/PNG export and output audits. `visual-adequacy.json` shows planned-versus-rendered marks and leaves readable-page inspection required. Keep the authored inspection record separate; never automatically infer designer approval from zero collisions. If a visual remains inadequate, repair the structure/content and rerender rather than hiding the issue in a QA score.

For complementary governance, use a peer triangle or actual collaboration network when there are reciprocal responsibilities; do not force a city→supplier→site hierarchy. Purchase demonstrations need the condition, device/field response and evidence to compare; a generic input photograph is not evidence of the whole test. Remove a speculative opportunity page when it duplicates a lifecycle/next-step decision rather than preserving the page count.
